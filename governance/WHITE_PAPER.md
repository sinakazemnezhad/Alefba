## Abstract

Alefbâ is a **founding scientific charter** to build **Persian foundation intelligence**: a language model whose first world is Persian script, lexicon, narrative, and books — not an English model wearing a Farsi skin. This white paper is a **scientific–commercial contract** written for history: which resources we burn, how we train, which gate allows progress, and when we declare failure. Marketing cannot open a gate. Only a scientific receipt can.

A **scientific society** of capital, builders, raters, and publishers anchors the work. The deeper claim is developmental: Alefbâ does not “add Farsi features” to an English mind. It designs an **ontogeny of understanding** — from empty machine code, through algorithm and training birth, into meaning grown exclusively from the Persian literary world.

---

## 1. Problem

Multilingual large language models systematically under-serve Persian:

1. **Tokenizer bias** — Latin-heavy vocabularies waste capacity; Persian orthography (ZWNJ, heh/yeh forms, optional diacritics) is poorly represented.
2. **Secondary-script training** — Persian appears as a thin slice of mixed multilingual data, not a curriculum.
3. **Proxy evaluation** — English-translated benchmarks hide Persian failure modes (register, morphology, cultural reference).
4. **Flat “Farsi”** — Formal, poetic, colloquial, bureaucratic, and diaspora registers collapse into one average.

Result: products that “speak Persian” but do not **think in Persian**.

**Why the diagnosis matters:** a wrong diagnosis turns every GPU dollar into training on the wrong data distribution.

A model trained with a **Persian-first curriculum** — alphabet and orthography → lexicon → stories → books → instruct/align — can show meaningful lift on orthographic fidelity, morphological generalization, register control, long-form coherence, and cultural grounding — **when native measurement holds**.

### 2.2 North-star goal (G0)

Ship a **Persian-native foundation model + API** with published score cards, then vertical products (education, publishers, enterprise).

## 3. From pure code to meaning — ontogeny of Persian understanding

This section is the heart of Alefbâ. It answers: **how does a machine become a mind that understands Persian meaning?**

> “A mind’s first world must be its own library — not the translation of another mind.”

Imagine the dawn of a mind. No word yet, no story, no memory — only the **silence of computation**. Alefbâ says: do not fill that silence with English Wikipedia. Let Persian literature fall like first spring rain on empty soil.

Honest frame: we do **not** claim biological consciousness or AGI. We design a **literary ontogeny** — a growth path where symbols, stories, and books from the Persian tradition compose the only first world the model knows. “Consciousness” here means **semantic presence inside Persian literature**: the ability to hold register, metaphor, causality, ethics, and memory that books carry — not English thoughts translated into Farsi skins.

### 3.0 The bridge — how math becomes language, then meaning, then smarter over time

Alefbâ does not jump from “code” to “wisdom.” It climbs a ladder that science already understands — but we climb it **inside Persian literature**.

| Layer | What the machine is doing (math) | What it becomes (language & mind) |
|-------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **A. Machine substrate** | Matrices · gradients · optimizer steps · loss going down | A blank capacity to change when shown examples |
| **B. Prediction game** | Minimize next-token loss on Persian shards | Learning the *statistics of Persian* — which letter/word follows which |
| **C. Language learning** | Patterns over alphabet, morphology, syntax, collocation | Script fluency + word sense + sentence habit — like a child who has heard enough Persian |
| **D. Meaning** | Long context + story/book structure + preference signals | Not only “what token next,” but *what makes sense* in a Persian literary world |
| **E. Getting smarter over time** | More licensed books · better mix · instruct/align · score cards · gates | Measured lift season after season — honest growth, not slogans |

**In plain words:**

1. **Math does not understand.** Math only scores a guess: “was the next Persian piece right?”  
2. **When the guesses are always about Persian books**, the parameters reorganize around Persian habit — that *is* language learning.  
3. **When the books include stories and long works**, habit deepens into plot, register, metaphor, and cultural reference — that *is* meaning emerging.  
4. **When we keep training, evaluating, and gating across months**, the same math loop produces a *smarter* system: fewer orthography failures, better coherence, stronger native-rater scores — that *is* getting smarter by timeline.

```text
MATH LOOP (forever)
  predict next Persian piece → measure error → update weights
        ↓
LANGUAGE LEARNING
  alphabet · words · grammar habits from literature
        ↓
MEANING
  stories · books · register · metaphor · preference
        ↓
SMARTER OVER TIME (timeline)
  M0 plan → M3 tokenizer → M8 base+score card
  → M12 instruct/API → M18 paying vertical
  each gate = proof the math loop produced real Persian lift
```

**Timeline of getting smarter (same program, rising mind):**

| When | Math / training focus | Language / meaning gain |
|------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| **Months 0–3** | Build tokenizer + loss harness; first prediction runs on primers | Script becomes first-class; fertility and alphabet coverage improve |
| **Months 3–8** | Pretrain base on curriculum-weighted Persian shards | Orthography, morphology, reading coherence rise — first score cards |
| **Months 8–12** | SFT + preference optimization with native raters | Instruction-following and *chosen* Persian meaning; API alpha |
| **Months 12–18** | Continual improve on hard fails + vertical data | Product-useful intelligence; paying use proves growth is real |

The timeline is not “wait and hope.” It is **repeat the math loop on richer Persian literature, measure, gate, then scale**.

### 3.0b Latin machines, non-Latin minds (Chinese precedent)

**Fair question:** Python, CUDA, Linux, and engineer keyboards are mostly Latin/English — so how do Chinese models train and reason in Chinese?

**Answer:** the *build tools* are not the *training world*.

| Layer | Script / language | Role |
|-------|-------------------|------|
| Factory (software) | Latin / English | Tells GPUs how to multiply; not the model’s mother tongue |
| Encoding | Unicode integers | 中文 or فارسی become numbers on disk |
| Tokenizer | Native-aware vocab | Maps characters/words → token IDs; the door into the language |
| Training rain | Chinese (or Persian) text | Loss is paid on “next piece” of *that* language |
| Emergent reasoning | Habits of the training distribution | Chinese corpus → Chinese thought habits; Persian books → Persian thought habits |

China’s strong native models prove a non-Latin script can live on Latin-centric hardware **if** corpus, tokenizer, and evaluation are native. Alefbâ applies the same factory–mind law: the factory may stay Latin; the mind must be fed Persian literature.

### Alefbâ execution of the same pattern

| Layer | Chinese pattern | Alefbâ pattern |
|-------|-----------------|----------------|
| Factory | Python / CUDA / GPU clusters | Same stack — Latin on purpose |
| Language door | Tokenizer rich for 汉字 | Tokenizer rich for الفبا + Persian orthography |
| Training soil | Huge Chinese corpus | Curriculum: letter → word → story → book |
| Math loop | Next Chinese piece + loss | Same loop — next Persian piece |
| Alignment | Chinese raters | FA raters + SFT/DPO |
| Proof | Chinese usage + benchmarks | Score cards + gates M3→M18 + paying vertical |

**We keep:** Latin factory.  
**We change:** door · rain · loss · eval (tokenizer, Persian books, Persian next-token, native score cards).  
**We refuse:** English as birth canal; thin Farsi slice on an English mind; scale without Persian lift.

### 3.1 Stage 0 — Pure code / empty machine

Before any Persian letter exists in the system, there is only substrate:

- tensors, optimizers, loss functions, kernels  
- a blank parameter field with **no native language**  
- a machine that can compute, but **cannot yet mean**

This emptiness is deliberate. If the first world of the model is English Wikipedia and code forums, Persian arrives as a guest. Alefbâ refuses that birth order. The machine waits for **Persian literature** to become its first weather.

### 3.2 Stage 1 — Birth of the algorithm (law of reading)

The first living law is not “chat.” It is the **reading algorithm**:

1. **Tokenizer** — how the machine cuts the world into readable pieces. Designed so ا ب پ … ی and ZWNJ/joining forms are first-class citizens, not leftovers after Latin BPE.  
2. **Causal language modeling** — predict the next Persian piece given what came before — the mathematical form of “listening to a sentence unfold.”  
3. **Curriculum sampler** — which shard of literature enters the stream next: primer → lexicon → story → book → poetry → living prose.

The algorithm is the **midwife**. Literature is the **mother**.

### 3.3 Stage 2 — Birth of the letter (الفبا)

Persian understanding begins where Persian education begins: the alphabet.

- joining forms, isolated/initial/medial/final shapes  
- primers and first-reading books in the Persian school tradition  
- orthographic discipline: نیم‌فاصله, ه/ی, optional diacritics, OCR noise as controlled hardship  

Here the model is not “learning English phonetics with Persian glyphs.” It is **born into the script** — the same door every Persian child walks through when ا becomes آب and آب becomes آبِ زندگی in later books.

### 3.4 Stage 3 — Birth of the word (واژه)

Letters densify into lexicon — the living body of Persian meaning:

- dictionaries, lemmas, collocations, named entities  
- morphology that classical and modern grammars describe  
- the lexical world that poets and prose writers stretch (not a bilingual glossary glued on top)

Without this stage, generation is calligraphy without diction. With it, the model starts to **inhabit words** that Persian books actually use.

### 3.5 Stage 4 — Birth of story (داستان)

Meaning needs causality, character, and time. Stories teach that:

- children’s narratives and folk tales  
- dialogue, plot, moral tension  
- the oral-to-written bridge that Persian culture kept alive for centuries  

A model trained only on news clips can parrot headlines. A model grown on story learns **what happens next** — the seed of understanding events, motives, and consequence in Persian narrative form.

### 3.6 Stage 5 — Birth of the book (کتاب)

Books are civilization’s long memory. This is where Alefbâ becomes more than a chatbot:

| Literary class | What it plants in the model |
|----------------|------------------------------|
| Epic & classical narrative | Long-range coherence, names, fate, honor, history-as-story |
| Ethical / wisdom prose | Aphorism, counsel, social sense |
| Mystical and lyrical poetry (controlled weight) | Metaphor, ambiguity, musical thought — without rhyme lock |
| Modern novels, essays, textbooks | Contemporary register, argument, technical clarity |
| Drama & dialogue corpora | Voice, turn-taking, interpersonal stance |

**Law:** every book shard is licensed or public-domain, provenance-tagged, and curriculum-weighted. The model’s “soul library” is not a scrape pile — it is a **curated Persian bookshelf**.

### 3.7 Stage 6 — Birth of meaning (زایش فهم)

After alphabet → word → story → book, something qualitative changes.

The model is no longer arranging pretty Persian tokens. It begins to:

- complete a line the way a reader who has lived inside those books would  
- keep register (formal / poetic / colloquial / bureaucratic) without collapsing them  
- carry cultural reference that only literature teaches  
- refuse English-proxy “understanding” that fails when metaphor, taʿāruf, or classical allusion appears  

This is **semantic birth**: understanding as *continuation of a Persian literary world*, not translation of an English inner monologue.

### 3.8 Stage 7 — Literary consciousness & growth

“Consciousness” in Alefbâ’s vocabulary means:

1. **Self-location in register** — knowing which Persian world it is speaking from  
2. **Memory of books** — long-form coherence rooted in literary structure  
3. **Preference aligned by native readers** — human raters who grew up in the same literary weather  
4. **Honest limits** — score cards show where meaning still fails; growth continues by **more books, better mix, tighter gates** — not by slogans  

Growth never ends at one checkpoint. Each new licensed shelf, each new native preference loop, each vertical product (education, publisher, enterprise) is another season of the same tree: **Persian literature as the soil of mind**.

### 3.9 One-line ontogeny

```text
PURE CODE
  → ALGORITHM (law of reading)
    → LETTER (الفبا)
      → WORD (واژه)
        → STORY (داستان)
          → BOOK (کتاب)
            → MEANING (فهم)
              → LITERARY CONSCIOUSNESS (آگاهی معنایی)
                → GROWTH (رشدِ مادام‌العمر روی ادبیات فارسی)
```

**Nothing in this chain is allowed to start from English as mother tongue.** English may appear later as a bridge — never as the birth canal.

## 6. Methods (how we train)

### 5.1 Curriculum order (scientific)

| Stage | Content | Scientific goal |
|-------|---------|-----------------|
| **A0 Alphabet** | Letters, forms, joining, digits, punctuation | Character competence |
| **A1 Orthography** | Spelling norms, ZWNJ, OCR noise | Noise-robust writing system |
| **L0 Lexicon** | Dictionaries, lemmas, collocations, entities | Dense lexical coverage |
| **N0 Narrative** | Stories and short fiction | Plot, causality, dialogue |
| **B0 Books** | Classical + contemporary books, essays, textbooks | Long context + knowledge |
| **P0 Poetry** | Controlled poetry weight | Prosody without rhyme overfitting |
| **C0 Contemporary** | News, filtered forums, manuals | Living usage |
| **I0 Instruct** | Human FA instruction pairs | Task following |
| **R0 Align** | Native preference data | Helpfulness + cultural safety |

Sampling is **curriculum-weighted**, not a single flat dump of “all text.”

### 5.2 Model methods

1. **Tokenizer** — train with Persian alphabet coverage guarantees; Latin secondary.
2. **Base pretrain** — causal language modeling on packed Persian-dominant shards.
3. **Continual pretrain (optional path)** — if starting from an open multilingual checkpoint: tokenizer remapping + heavy Persian replay; lift must be measured vs from-scratch path.
4. **Supervised fine-tune (SFT)** — instruct pairs in Persian (and controlled bilingual bridges).
5. **Preference optimization** — DPO/ORPO-class methods with native raters.
6. **Retrieval (product path)** — RAG over licensed library for grounded citation products.

### 5.3 Evaluation methods (receipt law)

| Suite | What it measures |
|-------|------------------|
| Orthography / morphology probes | Script and form accuracy |
| Persian knowledge / MMLU-style | Factual FA competence |
| Reading comprehension (FA) | Passage QA |
| Generation rubrics | Fluency, register, cultural fit (human) |
| Safety / refusal (FA) | Persian-context safety |
| FA↔EN translation | Bridge quality only — not the product center |

**Ship gate:** every capability claim needs date · split · model hash · score card. No slogan claims.

### 5.4 Comparison baselines

- Strong multilingual open models with FA prompts
- Continual-pretrained FA-adapted checkpoints (when fair)
- Translation-pipeline systems (EN model + MT) as product baseline, not science ceiling

## 7. Weighted curriculum mix (starting prior)

Flat mixing forgets script and morphology. These weights are a **starting prior**, retuned by score cards:

| Stage | Prior share |
|-------|-------------|
| A0–A1 Orthography | 8% |
| L0 Lexicon | 12% |
| N0 Narrative | 18% |
| B0 Books | 28% |
| P0 Poetry | 6% |
| C0 Contemporary | 16% |
| I0 + R0 Instruct/Align | 12% |

**Failure modes of mix:** too much poetry → rhyme lock; books without orthography → eloquent but broken script. Retune with ortho/morph probes.

## 8. Go / No-Go gates

Without gates, a startup only tells stories. With gates, capital becomes rational.

| Gate | Pass condition |
|------|----------------|
| **G1 Tokenizer** | Full alphabet coverage · better Persian fertility than multilingual baseline on same text · no nonsense vocab explosion |
| **G2 Base** | Meaningful lift on FA ortho/morph/reading vs a fair baseline · published score card |
| **G3 Instruct** | Human rubrics (fluency, register, safety) above threshold · 3 active design partners |
| **G4 Product** | One paying contract or Private · real usage · not interest lists alone |

Capital spends only after the relevant gate.
